\begin{abstract}
Federated learning (FL) enables collaborative medical risk prediction across decentralized hospital systems while maintaining data localization. However, classical deep learning architectures incur substantial communication overhead during distributed synchronization and remain vulnerable to membership inference and gradient inversion attacks. In this work, we propose, implement, and rigorously audit a privacy-preserving personalized hybrid quantum-classical federated learning framework for clinical intensive care unit (ICU) prognostication. The proposed architecture integrates a parameter-efficient Parameterized Quantum Circuit (PQC) bottleneck into a classical representation network, local hospital personalization adapters, formal record-level differential privacy (DP) via Rényi DP accounting, and a dropout-resilient four-round secure aggregation protocol. We evaluate the framework on a clinical 24-hour landmark cohort derived from the multicenter eICU Collaborative Research Database ($1,607$ encounters across $1,317$ unique patients), enforcing strict patient-disjoint partitions. Over an audited test set ($N=241$, $28$ deaths), the non-private hybrid quantum neural network (QNN) achieves an AUROC of $0.7658$, an AUPRC of $0.2957$, and a Platt-calibrated Brier score of $0.0980$, compared to $0.7166$, $0.2722$, and $0.0964$ for an equivalent classical multi-layer perceptron (MLP). A paired patient-clustered bootstrap analysis ($2,000$ replicates) establishes statistical parity without quantum superiority ($\Delta\text{AUROC} = +0.0491$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0409, 0.1393]$, $p=0.290$; $\Delta\text{AUPRC} = +0.0235$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0843, 0.1256]$, $p=0.624$). Crucially, the hybrid QNN requires only $957$ total trainable parameters (including $24$ variational angles in the quantum core) compared to $5,537$ parameters in the classical MLP ($82.7\%$ parameter reduction), yielding a $72.2\%$ reduction in federated communication payload ($0.80$~MB vs $2.88$~MB per round). Exploratory decision curve analysis confirms positive clinical net benefit over treat-all and treat-none baselines in the threshold range $p_t \in [0.09, 0.25]$. These results demonstrate that hybrid quantum federated learning provides communication-efficient, privacy-preserving clinical prognostication without predictive degradation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Clinical decision support systems deployed in intensive care units (ICUs) demand accurate, real-time risk stratification to anticipate physiological deterioration and optimize therapeutic interventions~\cite{rajkomar2018scalable,harutyunyan2019multitask}. While modern deep learning architectures demonstrate compelling prognostic discrimination on high-dimensional electronic health records (EHR)~\cite{johnson2016mimic,pollard2018eicu}, their development is severely constrained by healthcare data governance regulations, including the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and the European Union General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Centralized pooling of patient microdata across distinct hospital consortiums is frequently prohibited due to patient re-identification liabilities and institutional competitive barriers.

Federated learning (FL) has emerged as the leading distributed optimization paradigm to address institutional data silos~\cite{mcmahan2017communication,kairouz2021advances}. By transmitting model parameter updates rather than raw patient records, FL preserves data localization while enabling joint model training across clinical centers. Nevertheless, contemporary federated healthcare architectures face three fundamental methodological challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Communication Bottlenecks:} Deep classical networks comprise hundreds of thousands to millions of parameters. Synchronizing these dense gradient vectors over wide-area networks (WAN) with heterogeneous hospital edge servers creates unsustainable latency and bandwidth costs.
    \item \textbf{Privacy Leakage and Reconstruction Vulnerabilities:} Transmitting unmasked model weights or raw gradients does not ensure privacy. Gradient inversion attacks and membership inference attacks (MIA) can reconstruct private patient physiological trajectories from shared updates~\cite{abadi2016deep,bonawitz2017practical}.
    \item \textbf{Clinical Distribution Heterogeneity (Non-IID Data):} Institutional variations in patient demographics, disease acuity, and clinical documentation protocols induce severe statistical heterogeneity across hospital clients, impairing global convergence and degrading localized predictive calibration~\cite{li2020federated,vancalster2016calibration}.
\end{enumerate}

Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) deployed within hybrid quantum-classical neural networks offer a promising paradigm for compact high-dimensional representations in the noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) era~\cite{biamonte2017quantum,cerezo2021variational,bergholm2018pennylane}. Quantum variational cores utilize tensor product Hilbert spaces and non-local entangling gates to construct expressive representations with substantially fewer trainable parameters than classical deep networks~\cite{chen2021federated,shuld2019evaluating}.

In this paper, we propose, implement, and systematically evaluate an integrated \textbf{Privacy-Preserving Personalized Hybrid Quantum-Classical Federated Learning} architecture for ICU mortality prediction at a rigorous 24-hour clinical landmark. We explicitly ground our findings in certified empirical evidence from the eICU Collaborative Research Database~\cite{pollard2018eicu}, adhering to strict patient-disjoint partition standards and formal differential privacy accounting.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Federated Learning in Healthcare}
Federated Averaging (\textsf{FedAvg}) was formulated by McMahan et al.~\cite{mcmahan2017communication} as an iterative distributed optimization protocol aggregating client model weights weighted by local sample size. In clinical applications, severe inter-hospital non-IID heterogeneity frequently leads to client model drift. To stabilize convergence, Li et al.~\cite{li2020federated} introduced \textsf{FedProx}, incorporating a proximal regularization term $\frac{\mu}{2} \|\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{w}_{\text{global}}\|^2$ to penalize local updates that deviate excessively from the global objective. Subgroup personalization via isolated local adapters and transfer layers has gained prominence to address demographic distribution shifts without corrupting the shared global representation~\cite{kairouz2021advances}.

\subsection{Differential Privacy and Cryptographic Aggregation}
Formal privacy guarantees in machine learning are formalized through Differential Privacy (DP)~\cite{dwork2006calibrating}. Abadi et al.~\cite{abadi2016deep} established the foundational Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent (\textsf{DP-SGD}) algorithm, which clips per-sample gradients to a maximal $L_2$ threshold $C$ and injects calibrated Gaussian noise $\sigma C$. Mironov~\cite{mironov2017renyi} introduced Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP), providing analytically tight numerical composition under Poisson subsampling. In parallel, cryptographic Secure Aggregation (\textsf{SecAgg}) protocols, pioneered by Bonawitz et al.~\cite{bonawitz2017practical}, employ pairwise additive secret masks and Shamir's $(t, n)$ threshold secret sharing~\cite{shamir1979share} to allow the coordinating server to decrypt only the aggregate sum of client updates, concealing individual client vectors even in the presence of malicious servers and up to $40\%$ client dropout.

\subsection{Quantum Machine Learning and NISQ Algorithms}
Quantum machine learning exploits quantum mechanical phenomena---superposition, entanglement, and interference---to execute machine learning algorithms~\cite{biamonte2017quantum,cerezo2021variational}. Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs) encode classical data into quantum states via parameterized rotation gates, update variational parameters via classical gradient backpropagation (the parameter-shift rule), and read out expectation values of Pauli observables~\cite{bergholm2018pennylane,shuld2019evaluating}. Despite theoretical claims of quantum representational expressivity, high-dimensional barren plateaus~\cite{mcclean2018barren} and NISQ hardware decoherence restrict purely quantum models, establishing hybrid quantum-classical pipelines as the dominant viable framework~\cite{chen2021federated}.

\section{Research Gap and Contributions}
Prior literature in federated healthcare and quantum machine learning exhibits three critical methodological deficiencies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unsubstantiated Quantum Advantage Claims:} Numerous studies claim ``quantum supremacy'' or dramatic predictive leaps on tabular medical data without rigorous statistical significance testing, often ignoring dominant gradient-boosted decision tree baselines.
    \item \textbf{Inconsistent Privacy Accounting:} Many federated DP implementations evaluate heuristic record-level noise while asserting patient-level protection, or miscalculate Poisson subsampling rates by conflating expected batch sizes with nominal batch bounds.
    \item \textbf{Clinical Landmark Contamination:} Prior medical FL benchmarks often predict in-hospital mortality across arbitrary ICU stays without a fixed observation landmark, creating temporal target leakage and evaluation bias.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these gaps, our contributions are structured as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rigorous 24-Hour Clinical Landmark Cohort:} We extract and audit a standardized cohort of $1,607$ ICU stays ($1,317$ unique patients) from the multicenter eICU database, enforcing strict patient-disjoint partitions ($0.00\%$ patient overlap) and a 1,440-minute prediction landmark.
    \item \textbf{Compact Hybrid Quantum Variational Core:} We design a hybrid quantum neural network combining a classical bottleneck encoder with a 4-qubit, depth-2 Strongly Entangling circuit ($24$ variational angles, $957$ total trainable parameters), reducing parameter footprint by $82.7\%$ and federated transmission payload by $72.2\%$ compared to classical MLPs.
    \item \textbf{Certified Dual-Layer Defense:} We implement exact micro-batched per-sample DP-SGD tracked via Poisson-subsampled RDP accounting, coupled with a 4-round Shamir-based secure aggregation protocol achieving numerical reconstruction error $\le 1.73 \times 10^{-6}$ under up to $40\%$ client dropouts.
    \item \textbf{Definitive Patient-Clustered Statistical Audit:} Through $2,000$ patient-clustered paired bootstrap replicates, we demonstrate statistical parity between QNN and MLP baselines ($\Delta\text{AUROC} = +0.0491$, $p = 0.290$; $\Delta\text{AUPRC} = +0.0235$, $p = 0.624$), formally refuting quantum predictive superiority while validating computational feasibility.
    \item \textbf{Validation-Frozen Calibration and DCA:} We demonstrate that validation-fitted Platt scaling stabilizes neural predictions against extreme-risk tail overconfidence, verifying positive clinical net benefit across the threshold range $p_t \in [0.09, 0.25]$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proposed Hybrid Architecture}
The proposed hybrid quantum-classical architecture processes $D = 41$ standardized clinical features through three tightly integrated processing stages, depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}.

\subsection{Classical Bottleneck Dimension-Reduction Encoder}
Let $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{41}$ represent the standardized clinical feature vector of an ICU encounter. Direct amplitude or angle embedding of 41 continuous features into a quantum register would require either an impractically deep circuit or prohibitive qubit counts exceeding NISQ fidelity constraints. We formulate a classical bottleneck encoder $f_{\text{enc}}: \mathbb{R}^{41} \to \mathbb{R}^4$:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{h}_1 &= \text{GELU}(\text{GroupNorm}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}_1)) \\
    \mathbf{z} &= \tanh(\mathbf{W}_2 \mathbf{h}_1 + \mathbf{b}_2)
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{W}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{16 \times 41}$, $\mathbf{W}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 16}$, and $\text{GroupNorm}$ partitions the 16 channels into 2 groups to preserve sample-wise gradient isolation during differential privacy training (avoiding batch normalization target leakage). The output vector $\mathbf{z} \in [-1, 1]^4$ matches the 4-qubit register capacity.

\subsection{Parameterized Quantum Variational Circuit Core}
The quantum core operates on a register of $n = 4$ qubits initialized in the computational ground state $|0\rangle^{\otimes 4}$. The quantum transformation consists of two distinct stages, illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum_circuit}:

\subsubsection{Angle Feature Encoding}
The compressed continuous features $\mathbf{z} = [z_0, z_1, z_2, z_3]^T$ are mapped to single-qubit quantum states via rotation operators about the $Y$-axis:
\begin{equation}
    U_{\text{enc}}(\mathbf{z}) = \bigotimes_{j=0}^3 R_Y\left(\pi \cdot z_j\right)
\end{equation}
preparing the product state $|\psi_0(\mathbf{z})\rangle = U_{\text{enc}}(\mathbf{z}) |0\rangle^{\otimes 4}$.

\subsubsection{Strongly Entangling Variational Layers}
The state $|\psi_0\rangle$ is processed through $L = 2$ strongly entangling layers. Each layer $l \in \{1, 2\}$ applies universal single-qubit rotations followed by a cyclic entangling topology of CNOT gates:
\begin{equation}
    U_{\text{var}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \prod_{l=1}^L \left( U_{\text{ent}} \cdot \bigotimes_{j=0}^{n-1} R(\alpha_{j,l}, \beta_{j,l}, \gamma_{j,l}) \right)
\end{equation}
where $R(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = R_Z(\gamma) R_Y(\beta) R_Z(\alpha)$ denotes the arbitrary rotation, and the entangling unitary $U_{\text{ent}}$ is defined by cyclic controlled-NOT gates:
\begin{equation}
    U_{\text{ent}} = \prod_{j=0}^{n-1} \text{CNOT}_{(j, (j+1) \bmod n)}
\end{equation}
For $n = 4$ qubits and depth $L = 2$, the quantum variational core contains exactly $3 \times 4 \times 2 = \mathbf{24}$ trainable variational parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in [0, 2\pi]^{24}$.

\subsubsection{Multi-Observable Expectation Measurement}
Rather than reading out only computational basis probabilities, we extract rich non-linear representations by measuring the expectation values of both Pauli-$Z$ and Pauli-$X$ observables across all qubits:
\begin{equation}
    \langle Z_j \rangle = \langle \psi(\mathbf{z}, \boldsymbol{\theta}) | Z_j | \psi(\mathbf{z}, \boldsymbol{\theta}) \rangle, \quad
    \langle X_j \rangle = \langle \psi(\mathbf{z}, \boldsymbol{\theta}) | X_j | \psi(\mathbf{z}, \boldsymbol{\theta}) \rangle
\end{equation}
Concatenating these measurements yields an 8-dimensional quantum feature vector:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{q}(\mathbf{z}, \boldsymbol{\theta}) = \left[ \langle Z_0 \rangle, \dots, \langle Z_3 \rangle, \langle X_0 \rangle, \dots, \langle X_3 \rangle \right]^T \in [-1, 1]^8
\end{equation}

\subsection{Classical Post-Processing Classification Head}
The quantum measurement vector is projected to the final mortality prediction logit through a lightweight linear classifier $f_{\text{head}}: \mathbb{R}^8 \to \mathbb{R}$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{y}_{\text{logit}} = \mathbf{W}_4 \cdot \text{GELU}(\mathbf{W}_3 \mathbf{q} + \mathbf{b}_3) + b_4
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}_3 \in \mathbb{R}^{16 \times 8}$ and $\mathbf{W}_4 \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 16}$. The total number of trainable parameters in the complete hybrid QNN is exactly \textbf{957} ($760$ in the encoder, $24$ in the quantum core, and $173$ in the classification head).

\section{Dataset and Cohort Construction}
\subsection{24-Hour Clinical Prediction Landmark}
To prevent temporal leakage and establish clinically meaningful ICU prognostication, we formulate the primary clinical task as:
\begin{quote}
\textit{``Prediction of subsequent in-hospital mortality using clinical observations recorded strictly within the first 24 hours (1,440 minutes) of ICU admission.''}
\end{quote}
From the multicenter eICU Collaborative Research Database Demo v2.0.1~\cite{pollard2018eicu}, we extract patients admitted to ICU monitoring who survived the initial 24-hour observation window. Stays with missing discharge outcome records, observation durations under 24 hours, or death within the first 1,440 minutes were excluded.

\subsection{Strict Patient-Disjoint Cohort Partitioning}
To eliminate inter-split target contamination caused by repeated ICU encounters for the same patient, partitioning was executed strictly at the unique patient level (`\texttt{uniquepid}'). All encounters belonging to a given patient were assigned atomically to a single split.

\subsection{Standardized Feature Engineering}
A total of $D = 41$ clinical physiological features were engineered across four medical domains:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Vital Signs (24h Aggregates):} Heart rate, mean arterial blood pressure (MAP), systolic/diastolic blood pressure, respiratory rate, body temperature, and pulse oximetry ($\text{SpO}_2$) captured as minimum, maximum, mean, and standard deviation.
    \item \textbf{Laboratory Biomarkers:} Serum creatinine, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), serum glucose, sodium, potassium, bicarbonate, hematocrit, white blood cell (WBC) count, and blood lactate.
    \item \textbf{Neurological Acuity:} Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) total score and eye/verbal/motor subcomponents.
    \item \textbf{Demographics and Admission Characteristics:} Age, biological sex, admission body weight, emergency admission status, and pre-existing chronic comorbidities.
\end{itemize}
Missing values were imputed using median statistics fitted strictly on the training partition to prevent out-of-sample data leakage, followed by zero-mean unit-variance robust scaling.

\section{Privacy and Security Framework}
\subsection{Formal Record-Level Differential Privacy}
We guarantee rigorous mathematical privacy by training the models with Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent (\textsf{DP-SGD})~\cite{abadi2016deep} under Poisson subsampling. For an ICU dataset $\mathcal{D}$, privacy is formulated over neighboring datasets $\mathcal{D}, \mathcal{D}'$ differing by at most one clinical encounter record ($|\mathcal{D} \Delta \mathcal{D}'| = 1$):
\begin{equation}
    \Pr[\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}) \in \mathcal{S}] \le e^\varepsilon \Pr[\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}') \in \mathcal{S}] + \delta
\end{equation}

In each iteration $t$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A random batch $\mathcal{B}_t$ is sampled via Poisson trial with probability $q$. For QNN ($N_{\text{train}} = 1,132$), we set $q = 1/36$, yielding expected batch size $qN = 31.4444$. For MLP, $q = 1/18$, yielding $qN = 62.8889$.
    \item For each sample $i \in \mathcal{B}_t$, per-sample gradients $\mathbf{g}_i(\mathbf{w}_t)$ are computed via micro-batched backpropagation (using the exact parameter-shift rule on quantum circuits) and clipped to a maximum $L_2$ norm $C = 1.0$:
    \begin{equation}
        \bar{\mathbf{g}}_i(\mathbf{w}_t) = \mathbf{g}_i(\mathbf{w}_t) \cdot \min\left(1, \frac{C}{\|\mathbf{g}_i(\mathbf{w}_t)\|_2}\right)
    \end{equation}
    \item Perturbed aggregate gradients are generated by injecting calibrated spherical Gaussian noise:
    \begin{equation}
        \tilde{\mathbf{g}}_t = \frac{1}{qN} \left( \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}_t} \bar{\mathbf{g}}_i(\mathbf{w}_t) + \mathcal{N}\left(0, \sigma^2 C^2 \mathbf{I}\right) \right)
    \end{equation}
    Dividing by the constant expected batch size $qN$ preserves exact differential privacy invariance under random batch fluctuations.
    \item Total privacy expenditure $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ with target $\delta = 10^{-5}$ is numerically tracked across iterations using the Opacus Rényi Differential Privacy accountant~\cite{mironov2017renyi,yousefpour2021opacus}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dropout-Resilient Secure Aggregation}
To protect client update vectors during federated rounds from an untrusted coordinating server, we implement a four-round cryptographic Secure Aggregation protocol based on Bonawitz et al.~\cite{bonawitz2017practical}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Round 1 (Public Key Exchange):} Each client $u$ generates two keypairs: an ephemeral keypair $(c_u^{\text{pk}}, c_u^{\text{sk}})$ for Diffie-Hellman secret sharing, and a signature keypair for authentication.
    \item \textbf{Round 2 (Masked Input Generation):} Client $u$ computes pairwise shared keys $s_{u,v}$ with all participating clients $v$. It masks its local model weight vector $\mathbf{w}_u$ using additive pairwise masks and a private self-mask $b_u$:
    \begin{multline}
        \mathbf{y}_u = \mathbf{w}_u + \sum_{v > u} \text{PRG}(s_{u,v}) \\
        - \sum_{v < u} \text{PRG}(s_{v,u}) + \text{PRG}(b_u)
    \end{multline}
    Secret shares of $s_{u,v}$ and $b_u$ are distributed to peers using Shamir's $(t, n)$ secret sharing ($t = \lfloor 0.6n \rfloor$).
    \item \textbf{Round 3 (Consistency Check):} The server verifies threshold client participation.
    \item \textbf{Round 4 (Unmasking):} For surviving clients, peers reveal shares of $b_u$. For dropped clients, peers reveal shares of $s_{u,v}$. The pairwise masks cancel identically:
    \begin{multline}
        \sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{surv}}} \mathbf{y}_u - \sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{surv}}} \text{PRG}(b_u) \\
        - \sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{surv}}, v \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{drop}}} \text{PRG}(s_{u,v}) = \sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{surv}}} \mathbf{w}_u
    \end{multline}
\end{itemize}
In algorithmic validation experiments across 5 simulated consortium nodes with up to $40\%$ client dropouts, the numerical reconstruction root-mean-square error was verified to be strictly bounded by $\text{RMSE} \le 4.46 \times 10^{-7}$ ($\text{Max Absolute Error} \le 1.73 \times 10^{-6}$).

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\subsection{Federated Optimization Protocol}
We simulate a federated hospital network consisting of $K = 5$ distinct hospital consortium clients. Clinical non-IID data distribution across nodes was generated using a Dirichlet distribution $\text{Dir}(\alpha)$ with concentration parameter $\alpha = 0.5$.

Global models were trained across $R = 10$ communication rounds with $E = 2$ local epochs per round. Optimization utilized Adam with a base learning rate $\eta = 0.005$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$. Class imbalance was addressed via a cost-sensitive positive class weight $w_{\text{pos}} = 10.89$, derived from training mortality prevalence.

\subsection{Hospital-Level Personalization Adapters}
To resolve localized distribution shifts, clients retain localized adaptation layers while collaborating on global feature representations. Following federated aggregation, the global backbone is frozen, and hospital-specific adapter modules are fine-tuned locally for 5 epochs.

\section{Results and Statistical Analysis}
\subsection{Predictive Discrimination and Calibration Performance}
TABLE~\ref{tab:main_results} presents the authoritative evaluation metrics across all privacy budgets on the frozen test partition ($N=241$, $28$ deaths). Receiver Operating Characteristic and Precision-Recall trajectories are illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:roc_prc}.

In the unconstrained non-private regime ($\varepsilon = \infty$), the Hybrid QNN achieves an AUROC of \textbf{0.7658} and an AUPRC of \textbf{0.2957}, compared to \textbf{0.7166} and \textbf{0.2722} for the Classical MLP. 

Uncalibrated raw predictions reveal an important architectural difference: the classical MLP exhibits severe tail overconfidence on high-risk encounters, reflected in an elevated raw Brier score of $0.1621$. By contrast, the hybrid QNN exhibits inherently bounded outputs due to the compact trigonometric range $[-1, 1]$ of Pauli expectation measurements, yielding a significantly superior raw Brier score of $0.1106$ ($\Delta = -0.0516, p = 0.020$).

\subsection{Differential Privacy Utility Trade-Off}
As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:privacy_tradeoff}, both architectures demonstrate a characteristic monotonic decrease in predictive discrimination under increasingly stringent differential privacy bounds. At strict privacy ($\varepsilon = 1.0, \delta = 10^{-5}$), the Hybrid QNN maintains an AUROC of $0.5904$, while the Classical MLP drops to $0.5857$. At moderate privacy ($\varepsilon = 4.0$), the QNN recovers to an AUROC of $0.6410$ (AUPRC $0.1675$), while the MLP achieves $0.6016$ (AUPRC $0.1945$).

\subsection{Parameter Footprint and Communication Overhead}
The definitive advantage of the hybrid quantum architecture lies in its structural parameter efficiency. The classical MLP requires \textbf{5,537} trainable parameters across its dense layers ($[41 \to 64 \to 32 \to 16 \to 1]$). In contrast, the hybrid QNN requires only \textbf{957} total trainable parameters:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Classical encoder ($41 \to 16 \to 4$): $760$ parameters
    \item 4-qubit Strongly Entangling PQC: \textbf{24} variational angles
    \item Classical classification head ($8 \to 16 \to 1$): $173$ parameters
\end{itemize}

This represents an \textbf{82.7\% reduction} in trainable parameter footprint. In federated execution across 10 communication rounds, transmitting full model state dictionaries and optimizer momentum buffers requires $2.88$~MB per round for the Classical MLP, compared to only $0.80$~MB per round for the Hybrid QNN---a \textbf{72.2\% reduction in network transmission payload}.

\section{Calibration and Decision Curve Analysis}
\subsection{Two-Stage Calibration Protocol}
Clinical risk scores must output well-calibrated posterior probabilities and demonstrated clinical usefulness to support triage decisions~\cite{steyerberg2010assessing,vancalster2016calibration}. We implemented a strict two-stage calibration protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Validation-Split Fitting:} Platt scaling parameters~\cite{platt1999probabilistic} were fitted via logistic regression strictly on the independent validation split ($N=234, 24\text{ deaths}$), yielding slope $a_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{0.4415}$ and intercept $b_{\text{val}} = \mathbf{-0.0924}$ for QNN.
    \item \textbf{Out-of-Sample Application:} The fitted coefficients were frozen and applied out-of-sample to test logits: $\hat{p}_{\text{test}} = \sigma(0.4415 \cdot z_{\text{test}} - 0.0924)$.
\end{enumerate}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.74\columnwidth,height=1.65in,keepaspectratio]{figures/fig5_reliability_curves.png}
\caption{Reliability calibration curves on the test cohort comparing uncalibrated and validation Platt-scaled predictions against training and test prevalence baselines.}
\label{fig:calibration}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration} displays the resulting reliability diagrams. Both calibrated models strictly outperform the training prevalence baseline (Brier: $0.1041$) and the oracle test prevalence baseline (Brier: $0.1027$), achieving Platt Brier scores of $0.0980$ (QNN) and $0.0964$ (MLP) with reduced expected calibration error (ECE)~\cite{guo2017calibration}.

To evaluate out-of-sample calibration quality, a descriptive Cox calibration regression was fitted on test predictions, yielding calibration intercept $\alpha_{\text{test}} = 0.1081$ (ideal: $0$) and slope $\beta_{\text{test}} = \mathbf{1.7096}$ (ideal: $1$). We emphasize that $\beta_{\text{test}} = 1.7096$ is an out-of-sample descriptive evaluation diagnostic, completely distinct from the validation transformation parameter $a_{\text{val}} = 0.4415$.

\subsection{Exploratory Decision Curve Analysis (DCA)}

\begin{equation}
    \text{NB}(p_t) = \frac{\text{TP}}{N} - \frac{\text{FP}}{N} \left(\frac{p_t}{1 - p_t}\right)
\end{equation}

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Interpreting the Role of Quantum Circuits in Tabular EHR}
Our empirical findings provide clear guidance regarding the realistic deployment of variational quantum algorithms for tabular clinical risk prediction. Tabular electronic health records lack the continuous spatial or sequential translation invariances that favor specialized classical inductive biases (such as convolutional filters or attention heads). Gradient-boosted decision trees (e.g., XGBoost, achieving AUROC $0.8445$ on this benchmark) remain the empirical gold standard on tabular clinical data.

Within the class of differentiable neural architectures suitable for federated gradient aggregation and formal per-sample differential privacy, the hybrid QNN matches the predictive capacity of a substantially larger classical MLP while reducing parameter count by $82.7\%$. The quantum circuit functions as a highly expressive, parameter-compact periodic projection layer. By evaluating both Pauli-$Z$ and Pauli-$X$ expectations, the 4-qubit register maps 4-dimensional latent features into 8 mutually non-linear observables using only 24 variational angles.

\subsection{Privacy-Preserving Feasibility in Federated Healthcare}
The integration of exact micro-batched DP-SGD ensures certified defense against membership inference and model inversion attacks. However, the privacy-utility curve underscores the clinical cost of formal differential privacy: under strict guarantees ($\varepsilon = 1.0$), AUROC drops to $\approx 0.59$. This reveals that current localized DP mechanisms inject noise that disproportionately perturbs subtle clinical signals in low-prevalence mortality prediction ($8.77\%$ cohort prevalence). The four-round secure aggregation protocol provides critical complementary protection, ensuring that the central coordinator learns only aggregate model vectors.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
Several methodological limitations must be stated explicitly:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Single-Cohort Retrospective Evaluation:} The benchmark was constructed from the eICU Collaborative Research Database Demo v2.0.1. External multi-institution validation on MIMIC-IV or the full multicenter eICU database has not yet been executed and remains future work under appropriate Data Use Agreements.
    \item \textbf{Record-Level vs. Patient-Level DP:} Our formal differential privacy bounds apply at the clinical encounter level ($|\mathcal{D} \Delta \mathcal{D}'| = 1$). While patient disjointness is strictly maintained across cohort splits, multi-stay patients could theoretically weaken privacy guarantees if defined at the lifetime patient level.
    \item \textbf{Simulated Cryptographic Environment:} The secure aggregation protocol was evaluated algorithmically in an asynchronous simulated shared-memory runtime. Hardware latency, packet drops over wide-area hospital networks, and production TLS cryptographic handshakes were not evaluated.
    \item \textbf{Idealized Statevector Quantum Simulation:} Quantum circuits were simulated using ideal statevector mathematical execution (PennyLane `\texttt{default.qubit}'). Realization on physical NISQ quantum processors will encounter gate infidelities, readout errors, and thermal decoherence.
    \item \textbf{Exploratory Status of DCA:} Decision curve analysis was performed retrospectively on holdout test data; clinical deployment readiness or demonstrated improvements in patient survival outcomes cannot be inferred.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
This paper presented a privacy-preserving personalized hybrid quantum-classical federated learning framework for clinical ICU mortality prediction. Evaluated on a patient-disjoint 24-hour landmark cohort from the multicenter eICU database, the hybrid QNN achieves predictive discrimination and probability calibration on par with a classical MLP (AUROC $0.7658$ vs $0.7166$; paired bootstrap $p = 0.290$), while requiring $82.7\%$ fewer trainable parameters ($957$ vs $5,537$) and reducing federated communication payload by $72.2\%$. Integrated with certified record-level differential privacy and dropout-resilient secure aggregation, the framework resolves key distributed communication bottlenecks without sacrificing prognostic performance.

Future research will extend this methodology to external validation on the MIMIC-IV database, evaluate noisy quantum hardware backends on physical superconducting quantum processors, and investigate differential privacy mechanisms tailored to extreme class imbalance.
